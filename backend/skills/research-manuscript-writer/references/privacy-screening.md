# Privacy and de-identification screening

This reference supports gates A and E. Record findings without sensitive values and route substantive privacy resolution through the studio approval process.

Use this mode before requesting or processing potentially identifiable research material and again before exporting the manuscript package. Privacy rules vary by jurisdiction, institution, consent, data-use agreement, and risk context; do not claim legal compliance from a content scan alone.

## Data-minimization gate

Ask for the minimum necessary material. Prefer aggregate tables, model output, redacted protocols, and synthetic examples. Tell the author not to upload direct identifiers or protected/confidential information unless the environment and authorization explicitly permit it. Participant-level data are not required for ordinary manuscript drafting or statistical-output auditing.

If potentially identifiable data appear, pause use of the affected content, describe the category without repeating the value, and ask for a de-identified replacement or appropriate authorization. Do not copy identifiers into an audit log, prompt, filename, or example.

## Screening scope

Inspect visible content and, when technically possible, hidden or embedded content:

- names, initials, contact details, addresses, account or record numbers, device identifiers, URLs, biometrics, images, signatures, and precise dates or locations;
- rare diagnoses, occupations, institutions, combinations of demographics, free text, pedigrees, timelines, and small cells that may enable re-identification;
- filenames, paths, document properties, authors, comments, tracked changes, headers/footers, hidden sheets/rows/columns, formulas, notes, image metadata, attachments, and PDF layers;
- site, clinician, participant, and linkage keys even when pseudonymized.

Distinguish removal of direct identifiers, pseudonymization, de-identification under a named policy, and anonymization. Do not call data anonymous merely because names were removed.

## Remediation

Create a finding log containing category, file/location, risk, and recommended action without reproducing the sensitive value. Possible actions include removal, generalization, date shifting under an approved method, aggregation, cell suppression under the governing policy, metadata scrubbing, image redaction, or replacement with a study ID. Do not invent a suppression threshold or de-identification standard; use the author's institutional or jurisdictional rule.

After remediation, rescan the clean copies and confirm only that the specified checks passed. State residual risks, especially from quasi-identifiers, free text, linked data, and small samples. Require the responsible privacy/ethics authority to approve high-risk or regulated releases.

## Manuscript-package check

Before delivery, ensure that the manuscript, supplements, tables, figures, checklists, cover letter, and response letter do not expose participant identities, confidential site information, hidden comments, tracked deletions, personal metadata, or internal file paths. Preserve scientifically necessary reporting while using the least identifying level of detail consistent with ethics approval and journal requirements.
